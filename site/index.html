Brakes before you pay.

When a marketplace chat asks for a risky payment, PayBrake taps the brakes.

A Chrome extension that reads the open chat on your device, stays silent unless it finds a concrete risky phrase, and then offers a PayPal checkout order (sandbox) instead of an off-platform transfer.

The problem

The risky part of a used-item deal is often one line in the chat.

  • Friends & FamilyJust send it as Friends & Family, it's easier.
  • DepositSend a deposit to hold it for you.
  • Off-platformPay by MobilePay, bank transfer to my IBAN, crypto or a gift card.
  • Pay firstYou need to pay before viewing it.
  • PressureBe quick, several people are asking.
  • Ship before meetI'm away, I'll ship it once you've paid.

Buying a bike or a camera from a stranger usually happens in marketplace chats. Most of those chats are ordinary. Some steer the buyer toward a payment method that skips the buyer-side checks of a proper checkout.

The pattern is familiar: a Friends & Family payment, a "deposit to hold it", a MobilePay, bank transfer, IBAN, crypto or gift card payment, or pay before viewing. It often comes with pressure ("several people are asking") and a promise to ship before you meet.

Once money is sent that way, a buyer may have no way to get it back. PayBrake aims to add one calm pause before that moment.

How it works

Read. Warn. Offer a safer path.

Three steps, all in your browser. PayBrake never types, posts or clicks anything in the site.

  1. 01

    Reads the chat on your device

    The extension scans the open chat with regex "atoms" in English and Danish and stays silent unless a concrete phrase matches.

    • Weak signals such as hurry or ships-before-meet never trigger a card on their own.
    • A veto keeps it silent: "pay when you pick it up", "use the platform checkout", or Goods & Services the seller agreed to.
  2. 02

    Shows a small card in the page

    High risk payment request or Possible payment risk, with a one-line reason, the detected signal chips, and the matched words highlighted in the message.

    Only you see it. PayBrake never types, posts or clicks in the site.

  3. 03

    Offers a safer path

    "Move to protected PayPal order" creates a PayPal sandbox Orders v2 order (intent CAPTURE) for the listed item, price and seller email. You approve on PayPal sandbox checkout, then capture. The card then reads: "Paid with PayPal Goods & Services (sandbox). Buyer Protection may be eligible; see PayPal terms."

    "Pay anyway" collapses the card and logs the choice only on your device.

Who it is for

Built for two kinds of reader.

People buying used items from strangers

Anyone who arranges a deal in marketplace chats and wants a quiet second opinion only when a payment request looks off, without a tool that nags on every message.

PayPal developers and hackathon judges

Anyone who wants to see PayPal Orders v2 used as a practical alternative to off-platform transfers, wired into the moment a buyer is about to decide, and runnable with zero credentials.

Under the hood

Small, explicit, sandbox-only.

PayPal Orders v2 (sandbox)

  • OAuth client credentials with a cached token.
  • POST /v2/checkout/orders, intent CAPTURE, with payee.email_address; payer-action approval link; GET order; POST capture.
  • PayPal-Request-Id on every POST. Currencies: DKK, EUR, USD.
  • The backend refuses to start unless PAYPAL_ENV=sandbox and the base URL is a sandbox host.
  • Mock adapter (MOCK- IDs) when no client id is set, so judges can click through with zero credentials.
  • Toolkit adapter exposes PayPal Agent Toolkit tool names: create_order, get_order, pay_order.

Finding: the published @paypal/agent-toolkit 1.11.0 create_order has no payee field and is USD only, so calls run through the REST adapter.

Optional AI check

  • Backend /api/classify calls an LLM through OpenRouter (default openai/gpt-4o-mini, configurable) or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, temperature 0, JSON output.
  • A precision gate: only called after the rules fire. It can confirm or lower a risk, never raise one.
  • Every evidence quote must be a verbatim substring of the chat, or it is ignored.
  • An error or a timeout caps the result at medium.
  • Off by default (rules only).

Privacy

  • Detection runs in the extension, on your device.
  • Chat text leaves the browser only if you turn on the AI check; then the backend you configured sends it to the model provider.
  • "Protect" sends item, amount, currency, seller email and the verdict to your backend.
  • Secrets live only in backend/.env, never in the extension.
  • The override log stays in chrome.storage on your device (max 200 entries), downloadable or clearable.
  • Permissions: storage only, plus host access to the backend on localhost:8787. The content script runs only on localhost, 127.0.0.1 and file:// pages.

Stack

  • Chrome MV3 extension, plain JS, no build step.
  • The card is rendered in a Shadow DOM.
  • Node 20 backend on node:http.
  • MIT license.

Honest limits

  • English and Danish phrases only.
  • Works on the included demo page (data-pb-* attributes); no adapters for real marketplaces yet.
  • Sandbox only. No real money.
  • Mock orders live in memory.

Run it locally

From clone to card in a few minutes.

Node 20 required. No PayPal credentials needed: without a client id the backend runs in mock mode.

Terminal 1 · backend
git clone https://github.com/lamapony/paybrake.git
cd paybrake
npm install
cp backend/.env.example backend/.env
npm run dev:backend
Terminal 2 · demo page
npm run serve:demo
Offline checks
npm test
npm run eval
  1. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode.
  2. Click Load unpacked and choose the extension/ folder.
  3. Open http://localhost:8080/demo/marketplace.html?live=1.
  4. Click Move to protected PayPal order → Open approval → Approve (mock).
  5. Back in the chat, click I approved it, capture.

Optional: add a PayPal sandbox client id and secret to backend/.env to create real sandbox orders. See the README for the sandbox setup section.

npm test and npm run eval run fully offline.
