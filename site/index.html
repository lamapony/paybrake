Chrome extension · PayPal sandbox

The seller asks for Friends & Family. PayBrake offers a PayPal order instead.

PayBrake is a Chrome extension prototype. On our demo marketplace page it reads the open chat in your browser and shows a card when a message asks for Friends & Family, a deposit to hold the item, a transfer such as MobilePay, or a code or card details, or sends a suspicious payment link. The card offers a PayPal Orders v2 sandbox checkout instead.

People buy bikes and cameras from strangers through marketplace chats. Some sellers ask to be paid in a way that skips checkout.

The lines on the left are what PayBrake's rules match, in English and Danish. Codes, card details, suspicious links, fees via a link and remote-access apps are new in rules v3. Posing as a bank, hurry and ship before meeting only add to a warning. They never show a card on their own.

The rules only read the other person's messages, so your own reply never sets off a card. With the AI check on, an AI model also reads each chat and can add a card for scams the rules miss.

Money sent that way, or a code handed over, is hard to get back. PayBrake adds one card under the message that asked for it.

How it works

Rules find the request. You decide what happens next.

The rules in steps 1 and 2 run in your browser. With the AI check on and a server that has AI, a masked copy of the chat also goes to that server for a second opinion. Step 3 calls the backend, and only after you click. PayBrake does not type in the chat or click anything on the page.

  1. 01

    Reads the chat on your device

    The extension checks the open chat against regex "atoms" in English and Danish. No match, no card.

    • Weak signals like hurry or ships-before-meet never show a card on their own.
    • AI check: about a second after the chat stops changing, the AI's answer can add, keep or (only when very sure) remove the card. If it doesn't answer within 6 seconds, the card says "AI check unavailable" and the rules result stands.
    • A veto keeps it silent: "pay when you pick it up", "use the platform checkout", or Goods & Services the seller agreed to.
  2. 02

    Shows a card under the message

    High risk payment request or Possible payment risk, with a one-line reason, signal chips, and the matched words highlighted in the message.

    The card is drawn in your browser. The seller's side of the chat is unchanged.

  3. 03

    Offers a PayPal order instead

    "Switch to a PayPal order" creates a PayPal sandbox Orders v2 order (intent CAPTURE) for the item, price and seller email. You approve it on PayPal sandbox checkout, then capture. The card then reads: "Paid with PayPal Goods & Services (sandbox). Buyer Protection may be eligible; see PayPal terms."

    "Pay anyway" collapses the card and writes the choice to an override log on your device.

On public scam and normal messages

Held-out test split of 1,573 messages from four public datasets: real SMS, real Discord chat, and two synthetic sets (phone-scam dialogues and ScamGuardBench). None of them are marketplace chats. Rules v3 and the AI check (Typesafe jev-1.14.0, a yes/no decision model) were tuned on a separate dev split, then measured once on test. Every test message got an AI answer.

  • ~85%of scam messages got a card, rules + AI check (603 / 713)
  • ~1.6%of normal messages got a card, rules + AI check (14 / 860; 2 of the 14 are spam the dataset labels as normal)
  • 46%of scam messages got a card from the rules alone, with 0.8% of normal messages flagged
  • 10%normal messages flagged on ScamGuardBench's tricky "looks scary but legit" set (11 / 110)

On our own 30 synthetic chats

The team wrote these chats, with fictional names, and also wrote the rules. Rules mode only. A small set written by the same people says nothing about real-world accuracy.

  • 30synthetic chats we wrote
  • 16 / 16risky chats got a card (recall 1.0)
  • 0 / 14safe chats got a card (precision 1.0)
  • 150offline tests (npm test, branch eval-real-scams)

Who it's for

Meant for second-hand buyers. Usable today by PayPal developers.

People buying used items from strangers

The goal: a card appears only when the other person asks for a payment or details that are hard to take back, such as Friends & Family, a deposit, a transfer outside checkout, or a one-time code. Today it runs only on our demo page. There are no adapters for real marketplaces yet.

PayPal developers and hackathon judges

An Orders v2 integration started from a chat message instead of a checkout page. With no client id set, the backend runs in mock mode, so you can click through without a PayPal account.

Tech

Sandbox only, mock mode by default.

PayPal Orders v2 (sandbox)

  • OAuth client credentials with a cached token.
  • POST /v2/checkout/orders, intent CAPTURE, with payee.email_address; payer-action approval link; GET order; POST capture.
  • PayPal-Request-Id on every POST. Currencies: DKK, EUR, USD.
  • The backend refuses to start unless PAYPAL_ENV=sandbox and the base URL is a sandbox host.
  • Mock adapter (MOCK- ids) when no client id is set.
  • Toolkit adapter exposes PayPal Agent Toolkit tool names: create_order, get_order, pay_order.

Finding: the published @paypal/agent-toolkit 1.11.0 create_order has no payee field and is USD only, so calls run through the REST adapter.

AI check on all chats

  • Backend /api/classify asks Typesafe's decision model (jev-1.14.0, pinned) typed yes/no and pick-one questions: is the chat risky, plus extra checks for prize claims, premium-rate numbers and free-bait links. OpenRouter or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint can still be configured instead.
  • Runs on every chat, not only after the rules fire, so it can add a card the rules missed. It can remove a rules card only when it is very sure (confidence 0.85 or more).
  • Every evidence quote must be a verbatim substring of the other person's messages, or it is ignored.
  • No answer within 6 seconds, or an error: the rules result stands and the card says "AI check unavailable".
  • On by default in this demo build, and you can turn it off in Settings. Active on the hosted demo too (Typesafe), with a size cap and a per-IP rate limit.
  • In review on branch eval-real-scams; not in the v0.2.0-test.2 download yet. It costs about $0.07 per 1,000 checks, and an AI answer comes back in about 0.07 s (0.12 s at p95), measured from our server.

Privacy

  • The rules run in the extension, on your device.
  • With the AI check on (the default in this demo build), each chat goes to your configured server, which passes it to its AI provider. Before it leaves the browser, emails, phone numbers, card numbers, IBANs (all but the last 4), CPR and ID numbers, and one-time codes are masked. The other person's messages are sent, plus your newest message for context.
  • Names, street addresses and other free text are not masked. The masking is pattern-based and can miss things.
  • The hosted test server has AI (Typesafe) and masks the text again before passing it on. On a server without AI, no chat text is sent. With the AI check off, no chat text leaves the browser.
  • "Switch to a PayPal order" sends item, amount, currency, seller email and the verdict to the backend.
  • Secrets live only in backend/.env, never in the extension.
  • The override log stays in chrome.storage on your device (max 200 entries). You can download or clear it.
  • Permissions: storage only. Host access: the hosted demo server (paybrake-demo.spbabinov.workers.dev) and a local backend on localhost:8787. The content script runs only on the hosted demo, localhost, 127.0.0.1 and file:// pages.

Stack

  • Chrome MV3 extension, plain JS, no build step.
  • The card is rendered in a Shadow DOM.
  • Node 20 backend on node:http.
  • Hosted demo: a Cloudflare Worker with PayPal in mock mode (no PayPal keys) and the Typesafe AI check. Test orders are kept 24 hours and pages are sent with noindex.
  • MIT license.

Limits

  • English and Danish phrases only.
  • The AI check flags some normal messages: about 1.6% on our public test data, more on family money requests and bank notices.
  • Works on our demo page (data-pb-* attributes). No adapters for real marketplaces yet.
  • Sandbox only. No real money.
  • Local mock orders live in memory. The hosted demo keeps test orders for 24 hours.

Install

Try the test build in about 2 minutes.

Recommended: the v0.2.0-test.2 pre-release. It is a mock build, so you don't need Node, a server or PayPal credentials. The repo is private for now, so the release link only works if you have repo access.

  1. Download paybrake-extension.zip from the v0.2.0-test.2 pre-release and unzip it.
  2. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode.
  3. Click Load unpacked and pick the paybrake-extension folder (the one with manifest.json).
  4. A welcome tab opens. Click Try the demo store, or open the hosted demo.
  5. In the bike chat: Switch to a PayPal order → Open approval → Approve (mock). Back in the chat, click I approved it, capture. Then open the camera chat and click Pay anyway. The desk lamp chat gets no card.
Get v0.2.0-test.2 Open the hosted demo

The test build talks to the hosted mock server by default. To use your own backend, set the Backend URL in Settings to http://localhost:8787.

If the hosted demo shows a yellow "PayBrake is not running in this browser" box, the extension isn't loaded in that Chrome profile.
